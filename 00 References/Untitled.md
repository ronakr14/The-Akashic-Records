**PII (Personally Identifiable Information)** and **PHI (Protected Health Information)** are related but not the same.

|Aspect|PII|PHI|
|---|---|---|
|Definition|Information that can identify a person|Health-related information that identifies a person|
|Scope|Any industry|Healthcare and healthcare-related organizations|
|Regulation (U.S.)|Various laws (state privacy laws, FTC rules, etc.)|Primarily governed by HIPAA|
|Examples|Name, email, phone number, SSN, driver's license number|Medical record number, diagnosis, treatment information, lab results linked to a person|
|Protection Required|Usually yes, depending on law and context|Always requires strict safeguards under HIPAA when held by covered entities/business associates|

### Examples of PII

- Full name
    
- Email address
    
- Phone number
    
- Social Security Number (SSN)
    
- Passport number
    
- Home address
    
- Employee ID
    

### Examples of PHI

- Patient name + diagnosis
    
- Medical record number
    
- Lab results
    
- Prescription information
    
- Insurance member ID linked to healthcare records
    
- Appointment records
    
- Medical images associated with a patient
    

### Relationship Between PII and PHI

- **All PHI contains identifying information and health information.**
    
- **Not all PII is PHI.**
    

For example:

|Data|PII?|PHI?|
|---|---|---|
|John Smith's email address|Yes|No|
|John Smith's blood test result|Yes|Yes|
|Anonymous blood test result with no identifiers|No|Usually No|
|Patient ID linked to medical records|Yes|Yes|

### Easy Rule to Remember

- **PII = "Who is the person?"**
    
- **PHI = "Who is the person + what is their health information?"**
    

In healthcare compliance, PHI is generally treated as a more sensitive subset of personal information because it involves both identity and medical data.
