## 1. Shift from "technology collector" to "system builder" ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This is the biggest thing I've noticed.

You explore a lot:

- AI agents
    
- PKM
    
- Obsidian
    
- LLM frameworks
    
- GitHub repositories
    
- Data Engineering tools
    
- Open-source projects
    

That's a strength—but it can become "tool tourism."

Instead of asking:

> "What does this repository do?"

Start asking:

> "How would I combine this with five other ideas into something people actually use?"

Architects build systems. They don't just know technologies.

## 2. Write much more ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

I know you maintain a PKM repository for career growth and learning. That's a great foundation.

The next step is to make your thinking visible.

Examples:

- Architecture decision records
    
- Deep technical articles
    
- "Why I designed it this way"
    
- Reverse engineering popular systems
    
- Trade-off analyses
    

Writing forces clarity.

By 2030 (staying in our roleplay), AI generated plenty of code. Original thinking became much rarer.

## 3. Become exceptional at architecture ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

You mentioned your long-term goal is architecture.

So don't just ask:

> "How does Snowflake work?"

Ask:

- Why Snowflake instead of BigQuery?
    
- Why Kafka instead of Event Hubs?
     
- Why batch instead of streaming?
    
- Where does this fail?
    
- What happens at 10× scale?
    

Architects optimize trade-offs, not syntax.

---

## 4. Improve influence, not just implementation ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

Senior engineers eventually spend less time coding and more time helping others make good decisions.

Practice:

- Explaining complex ideas simply
    
- Leading design reviews
    
- Giving technical feedback
    
- Presenting architecture diagrams
    
- Writing persuasive design docs
    

These skills scale your impact.

---

## 5. Develop stronger product thinking ⭐⭐⭐⭐☆

A lot of engineers think:

> "Can I build it?"

The next question is:

> "Should anyone use it?"

When evaluating any project, ask:

- Who benefits?
    
- What pain does it solve?
    
- Why now?
    
- Why is it better?
    
- What's the business value?
    

Technical excellence plus product thinking is a powerful combination.

---

## 8. Spend less time consuming, more time creating ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This is the advice I'd emphasize most.

A useful rule of thumb:

> For every 1 hour spent learning, spend 2 hours building.

That forces ideas to become practical experience.
